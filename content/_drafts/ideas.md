### The 3Ws Cost You Customers
- **Premise:** A mower repair shop skipped a scheduled pickup over a missed call, then said they'd let me know if they were "still interested." Being at capacity isn't a scheduling problem, it's the most important fact about your business, and silence hands the job to your competitor.
- **Gates:** Relatable ✓ (anyone who has hired a small business and been left in silence) · Transformative ✓ (four numbers to count, three Monday moves) · Share-worthy ✓ ("still interested in picking it up" is the quotable line)
- **Verdict:** Drafted — `the-3ws-cost-you-customers/index.md`
- **Note:** Returns 3Ws to their original customer-facing meaning (Janda), which the blog has drifted toward team/project use.
- **Open gap:** gross/net numbers for a mower repair shop, if Chance found real ones. TODO marker in draft.
- **Captured:** 2026-07-29

### What Are Your Non-Negotiables at Home?
- **Premise:** Chance asks people what their household non-negotiables are, because a constraint set ahead of time removes the daily decision. His list: garage stays empty enough to park in, no eating in the van, nothing goes in the attic, no shoes past the stairs, food stays in the kitchen (except movie night). The mechanism underneath all of them: make the right thing easy and the wrong thing hard.
- **Gates:** Relatable ~ (home examples land, but "anyone overwhelmed at home or work" is too broad and skips the tech-lead reader) · Transformative ~ (mechanism is real but currently buried under examples) · Share-worthy ✓ (question-format title invites people to answer with their own list, which is exactly how this spreads for Chance in person)
- **Verdict:** Needs sharpening
- **Sharpening notes:**
  - Lead with the mechanism, not the list. Examples are proof, not the argument.
  - Coworker's rule belongs here: buy fewer flat surfaces, because flat surfaces accumulate. Constraint applied at purchase time, not cleanup time.
  - Link to `blog/focus-get-closer-to-zero/` (2021) for the "would you rather clean 100 items or zero" conversation with the boys.
- **Open questions blocking the draft:**
  - Two constraints Chance actually enforces on his team at work, real ones he would defend in a retro. Without these the post never reaches the blog's reader.
  - One constraint he broke or dropped, and what happened. Every example is currently a win, which reads like a brag list.
- **Alt titles considered:** Helpful Constraints · Put Constraints to Work for You · Non-Negotiables
- **Captured:** 2026-08-14

### An Empty Surface Is a Clean Surface
- **Premise:** "Clean" is subjective, so it gets negotiated and it slips. "Empty" is binary, so it does not. Setting the countertop target to zero items gives every item a home, makes the mess obvious, and turns cleanliness into a definition of done rather than a judgment call.
- **Gates:** Relatable ✓ (anyone who has argued with themselves about whether a room counts as clean) · Transformative ✓ (replaces a subjective standard with a binary one) · Share-worthy ✓ (title is the quotable line)
- **Verdict:** Needs sharpening
- **Sharpening notes:**
  - This is the bridge to the tech-lead reader. Same trick in a different room: WIP limits, a lint rule that fails the build instead of a style guide nobody reads, no TODOs merged to main, empty inbox as the done state.
  - Source spark was a YouTuber with a zero-item countertop. Chance found the explanation vague; the fix is naming *why* it works, which is the binary state.
  - Ends on the hotel room: nothing demanding your attention, no task list, no regret from your past self. Emptiness read as a message from whoever prepared the space.
- **Open question:** Does this stand alone, or is it the second half of the non-negotiables post? Decide before drafting.
- **Captured:** 2026-08-14

### Earn the Right to Consume
- **Premise:** The Smith house rule was that fun purchases have to be earned with something other than money, and digital consumables (game tokens, coins) were off the table because they vanish and cannot be resold. Then Chance changed the rule: if the kids split money into give/save/spend, the spend bucket is theirs to burn however they want.
- **Gates:** Relatable ✓ (any parent staring down an in-app purchase request) · Transformative ✓ (a specific rule, plus permission to loosen it) · Share-worthy ✓ (the reversal is the hook, most parenting-rule content never admits a rule changed)
- **Verdict:** Needs sharpening
- **Sharpening notes:**
  - Split out of the constraints dump on purpose. The other constraints reduce friction; this one adds friction deliberately. Different mechanism, different post.
  - The reversal is the story. Do not present the original rule as the answer.
- **Open questions blocking the draft:**
  - Why did Chance loosen the rule? What was he actually protecting, and did he decide it was the wrong thing to protect?
  - What did the kids do with the freedom once they had it?
- **Captured:** 2026-08-14
