# The Simple Church Website Manifesto

A church site is required. It is not the priority.

It is an accessory to the mission of outreach — not the mission itself. The moment a website becomes a project that consumes staff attention and budget, it has gotten too big for its job.

Here is what we believe about helping a church get online.

---

**The church owns the code.**

Not a platform. Not a vendor. The actual files. This matters because ownership means anyone — including an AI — can help update it later. Getting locked into a framework or tool that requires upgrades and monthly costs is trading one problem for another.

**The church owns the accounts.**

Domain registrar. Hosting. Repository. Every login. If the volunteer who helped disappears, the church should not disappear from the internet with them.

**No frameworks. No monthly costs.**

There are tools that make it easy to launch a website and easy to become dependent on them. Avoid them. Keep the stack simple enough that it does not go out of date quickly and does not require a subscription to keep the lights on.

**Start with content only.**

Correct content. Targeted at people looking to visit or join. Not colors, not imagery, not features — not yet. A plain site that tells the truth about who you are and where you meet is better than a beautiful site that is six months out of date.

**AI is the ongoing editor.**

When the site is set up right, a church admin can describe a change to an AI and the site gets updated. No developer required. That is the goal. Not a handoff to helplessness — a handoff to capability.

**Documentation ships with the site.**

Anyone who comes after should be able to pick it up. Screencasts, written steps, a README. The sprint is not done until the teaching is done.

**Keep it simple so it doesn't go out of date quickly.**

The more complex the site, the higher the maintenance ceiling. A simple site can be kept current by people who are not developers.
