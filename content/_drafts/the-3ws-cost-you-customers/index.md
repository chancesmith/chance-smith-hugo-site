My mower has an oil leak and the right wheel drive quit pulling.

I found a local repair shop that picks up for free on Tuesdays. Free pickup. That is a real offer, and it is the reason I picked them.

Tuesday morning at 7:39 AM, they called twice.

I saw both calls and ignored them. I assumed robocall. I figured that if a human wanted me, a human would leave a voicemail or send a text.

That was my mistake. I am still kicking myself for not calling back when I saw the missed calls.

At lunch it hit me. That was probably the mower shop. It was.

## Nothing happened, and nothing told me

They did not come.

No voicemail. No text. No note on the door. The pickup I had scheduled quietly stopped existing, and the way I found out was by watching my driveway stay empty.

So I called them.

They confirmed they had tried to reach me, and said they would need to get the mower the following Tuesday. Then this:

> They would call me Friday to let me know if they were still interested in picking it up.

Interested.

I am going to be generous and call that a bad choice of words. I do not think they meant it the way it landed. I think they are buried, and Tuesday pickups are hard to staff, and they already have more mowers in the shop than they can turn around.

But look at what that sentence does to a customer. I now spend three days doing all three Ws. I wait on a call. I worry the mower is not getting fixed before the grass wins. I wonder whether I should be calling somebody else.

The [3Ws](/glossary#3Ws) are wait, worry, wonder. I usually write about them for teams and projects. Michael Janda aimed them at customers first, and this is why.

## Being full is a decision you are making by accident

Here is what I would want to know if this were my shop.

Am I actually out of capacity, or does it only feel that way?

Because if I am out of capacity, that is not a scheduling problem. That is the single most important fact about my business, and it changes what I do next. When demand is higher than what I can deliver, the answer is not to work faster. The answer is to raise prices, deliver more of what customers will actually pay for, and use that margin to hire someone worth their time and worth it to the business.

You cannot make that call on a feeling. You need four numbers, and none of them require software.

1. Calls for new work per week
2. How many I turned down or could not service
3. New customers per week
4. Jobs completed per week

Number two is the one nobody tracks, and it is the one that pays for the hire.

Right now this shop has a leaky bucket. There is a gap between the customers who want to pay them and the customers they can actually serve, and every week that gap goes unmeasured, the leak looks like a busy season instead of a business decision.

## You are handing your competition the easy win

Every W I spent on this shop was an opening for the next shop in the search results.

I have called around to a lot of small businesses in my area. Communication is the most common thing they are bad at, and it is the cheapest thing to fix. A text costs nothing. A voicemail costs nothing. Telling me the truth about next Tuesday costs nothing.

Nobody has ever told me to stop sending them updates. Not once. People can subscribe to as much or as little as they want, and being able to search back later for what was said is a gift to both of us.

If I ran the shop, Monday looks like this:

- **Text before you call.** "It's the mower shop, here for your Tuesday pickup, on my way by 8." Now a missed call is not a canceled job.
- **Never let a plan change in silence.** If the pickup is off, say so in writing before the window closes. The customer finding out by absence is the worst version of every outcome.
- **Count the noes.** One line in a notebook every time you turn work away. Read it at the end of the month and let it tell you whether to hire.

None of that is a system. It is a phone and a habit.

## The point is

The mower is a small thing. The pattern is not.

You do not lose customers because you are full. You lose them because you left them waiting, worrying, and wondering while you were full, and someone down the road just picked up the phone.

Also, call people back when you see the missed call. I am telling myself.
