## The Goal

Three things. In two days. For free.

1. Replace the current site with a simple site that can change over time
2. Ensure the church owns the accounts and the code
3. Leave documentation that teaches anyone how to make future changes

---

## Stage One: Simple Site

Create a simple layout with content only. The content needs to be helpful, correct, and targeted at people looking to visit or join.

The constraints: **correct content and a simple layout only** — not colors, not imagery, not features. It will look plain. That is intentional. This is a first iteration.

The site should be built in a way that AI can help anyone update it. Avoid frameworks. Avoid tools that require upgrades or monthly costs. Own the code.

## Stage Two: Domain Forwarding

Set up hosting for the domain. If the church has an old domain, forward traffic from the old domain to the new one.

Old domain → New domain

If there is a name change or rebrand, add a landing page at the old domain that communicates the change before redirecting. This protects people who have the old address bookmarked.

---

## Stage Three: Transfer Ownership

Before you leave, make sure the church has full ownership and access to:

- Domain registrar (e.g., GoDaddy)
- Repository (e.g., GitHub)
- Hosting (e.g., Netlify)

Every login. Every account. The church should be able to operate without you the day after you finish.

Set a goal with your contact: transfer everything to a church-owned email address. Not a personal one.

---

## Stage Four: Teaching

The sprint is not done when the site is live. It is done when someone at the church can make a change without calling you.

Document:
- How to make common changes
- What the workflow looks like (describe a change to AI → AI edits the files → site updates)
- What the future looks like (adding images, colors, staff pages, events — all possible, all iterative)

Add documentation to GitHub. Record screencasts if you can.

---

## TL;DR

Two days. Four stages.

1. Simple new site — no framework, no monthly costs
2. Domain forwarding — old address routes to new
3. Full ownership transferred — every account, every login
4. Teaching complete — someone at the church can make changes

When it is done, people can arrive at a website that tells them who you are and gives them what they need to visit.

---

## What Comes After

The sprint ends. The site does not.

The church can now add images, colors, a staff list, events, sermons, live streams, and ministry pages — iteratively, over time. Whether changes are made by hand or by asking AI to do it, the foundation is there.

The finish line for your involvement is Stage Four. After that, you have given them something they can grow.
